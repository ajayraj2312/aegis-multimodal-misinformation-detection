System architecture7 layers
1 · Data acquisition & preprocessing — text is validated, trimmed and WordPiece-tokenised; images are decoded, converted to RGB, resized to 224×224 and normalised with ImageNet statistics.
2 · Text feature extraction — frozen DistilBERT-base-uncased; the masked mean of the last hidden state gives a 768-d contextual embedding.
3 · Image feature extraction — frozen ResNet18 (ImageNet weights) with the classifier head removed; global average pooling gives a 512-d visual embedding.
4 · Projection — a two-layer MLP per modality maps both into a shared 256-d, L2-normalised space.
5 · Cross-modal consistency — cosine similarity between the projected text and image vectors, plus a dedicated consistency head that consumes the fused representation.
6 · Fusion — learned modality gates weight each projection; the gated concatenation is passed through a 512 → 256 → 64 MLP.
7 · Classification — a linear head outputs Credible / Misleading; the softmax probability is reported as the confidence score.
Training protocol
Optimiser
AdamW, lr 1.2e-3, cosine schedule
Loss
class-weighted cross-entropy + 0.35 × consistency CE
Batching / epochs
48 / up to 80 with early stopping
Validation
15% stratified split, loss-based checkpointing
Threshold
F1-optimal on validation, then frozen
Reported metrics
computed once on the 15% test split
Encoders
frozen — only the fusion/baseline heads are trained

Because the encoders are frozen, the fusion head is trained on cached embeddings. This keeps training to seconds, is fully reproducible with a fixed seed, and makes it easy to swap in a stronger encoder later — but it also means the encoders are not fine-tuned on this task.

What the labels mean
A post is labelled Misleading when the claim text itself carries misinformation signals, or when the attached image does not match the topic the text describes. It is labelled Credible when the text is credible and the image topic matches — and also for 22% of credible-text posts whose image is deliberately mismatched, because an illustrative or stock image is not by itself misinformation. That deliberate ambiguity is what stops the benchmark from being trivially separable and is why no model can reach a perfect score on it.
The consequence is intentional: a text-only model cannot separate a genuine claim from the same claim paired with an unrelated image, because nothing in the text looks wrong. That is precisely the gap that motivates the multimodal design, and it shows up as the recall gap on the evaluation page (0.800 text-only vs 0.967 fusion). Half of the mismatched images also come from visually adjacent topics, and 12% of images are heavily degraded, so the visual branch cannot simply memorise six scene categories.
Limitations — read before quoting any number

1. Proxy benchmark. The shipped benchmark is built from curated claim texts and procedurally rendered topic scenes. It proves the pipeline learns and that fusion helps on this corpus. It does not establish real-world accuracy, and the numbers must not be presented as benchmark-comparable to Fakeddit/Weibo results.

2. No manipulation forensics. ResNet18 is a generic image classifier backbone. It cannot prove an image was edited or AI-generated. The "visual artefact indicators" on the result page are transparent pixel statistics (overlay-band edge density, JPEG quantisation estimate, blockiness) and are labelled as heuristics.

3. Confidence ≠ truth. A 92% score means the network is confident about its own decision boundary, not that a fact-checker has verified the claim. Out-of-distribution posts can be confidently wrong.

4. Frozen encoders. Fine-tuning DistilBERT and ResNet18 on a large real corpus (e.g. Fakeddit, MM-COVID, Weibo) is the natural next step and is expected to change these numbers substantially.

5. Language and scope. The text encoder is English-only (uncased DistilBERT) and the pipeline handles a single image with no social context (shares, accounts, propagation).

6. Storage on free hosting. SQLite and uploaded images live on the instance filesystem. Render's free tier has an ephemeral disk, so records are lost on redeploy unless a persistent disk is attached.

Retraining & extending
python -m src.train # build features, split, train 3 heads
python -m src.evaluate # write models/metrics.json for this page
python scripts/seed_demo.py # optional: real pipeline runs over sample posts

To use your own corpus, replace src/benchmark.py with a loader that returns the same sample dicts (text, text_topic, image_topic, label, consistency, image_path) and re-run the two commands. Nothing else in the pipeline needs to change.
